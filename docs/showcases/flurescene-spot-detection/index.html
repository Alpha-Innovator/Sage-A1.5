A field of faint signals —
which ones are real spots?

Fluorescence images contain small bright signals against an uneven background. Finding their centers means deciding what is a spot, what is noise, and how that decision changes from one dataset to another.

About this research
QUESTION, APPROACH AND EVIDENCE

How can a detector improve
across different kinds of images?

The recorded study combines U-Net detection with a Laplacian-of-Gaussian fallback, then revisits peak thresholds, test-time augmentation, training and preprocessing in response to evaluation feedback. All eight submissions and 72 original F1 scores are retained.

What the evidence shows

Optimization result

The confirmed aggregate endpoints are -0.0469 and 0.1158, an absolute increase of 0.1627. Corrected intermediate aggregate scores were not supplied; the evaluation chart therefore uses original per-dataset F1 scores.

Prediction comparison

The first test fields from DNA-FISH, Telomeres, HybISS and MERFISH come from the same separate 20261015 seed-1 run. They are not assigned to optimization submissions. Selection follows filename order, before inspecting scores. The supplied DNA-FISH comparison is unchanged. For the additional fields, stored coordinates are matched with the same Hungarian rule and 3-pixel cutoff; both panels share a linear contrast mapping from the image minimum to its 99.8th percentile. These metrics describe individual images, not whole datasets.

Process animation

The 32-second animation reconstructs the recorded method changes and evaluation results. Diagrams illustrate the method; the numbers are original scores. It is not a screen recording, a new training run or a reconstruction of unavailable intermediate images.
